# SFFA v. Harvard — primary documents on enrollment forecasts

Court documents and expert reports from *Students for Fair Admissions, Inc. v.
President and Fellows of Harvard College* (and the consolidated UNC case) that
contain quantitative forecasts of how Black, Hispanic, Asian-American, or
Native American enrollment would change at elite colleges if race-conscious
admissions were eliminated. Where possible the file is the *original* source
of the claim, not a brief quoting that source.

The Amherst-led brief and the UC brief are amicus filings, but they are
included because they are the *original* public source for institution-specific
forecasts that no underlying document expresses (Amherst's internal
race-blind modeling; the UC system's authoritative record of post-Prop 209
outcomes). The 15-research-university brief is included for the *contrast*:
those amici defended affirmative action without disclosing institution-specific
simulations of their own — a notable absence given their resources. The
Harvard merits brief, the Solicitor General's brief, the HBCU brief, and the
AERA brief — which all quote the documents above rather than making original
forecasts — are not included.

## Key forecasts, with quotation and citation

### File 01 — District court opinion (Burroughs, J.)

The trial court's headline finding on what would happen if Harvard simply
stopped considering race, holding everything else constant. This is the
finding that subsequent briefs across the case all cite back to.

> "In the absence of any other adjustments to Harvard's admissions policy,
> eliminating consideration of race would cause the African American
> representation at Harvard to decline from approximately 14% to 6% of the
> student population and Hispanic representation to decline from 14% to 9%.
> Over the course of four years, the number of African American and Hispanic
> students at Harvard would fall by nearly 1,000 students."
>
> — *SFFA v. Harvard*, 397 F. Supp. 3d 126, [`01_district_court_opinion_burroughs_2019.pdf`](01_district_court_opinion_burroughs_2019.pdf) **at 84** (Burroughs, J., D. Mass. Sep. 30, 2019).

A second variant, attributing a slightly larger projected drop to Card's
modeling once legacy/donor/child-of-faculty preferences are also removed:

> "Professor Card reasonably estimated that eliminating tips for race and
> ALDC status, along with eliminating deferred admissions, would cause African
> American enrollment to decline from 14% to 5% and Hispanic enrollment to
> decline from 14% to 9%."
>
> — *Id.* **at 86**.

On the SFFA-preferred remedy ("Simulation D"):

> "Mr. Kahlenberg's simulations uniformly suggest that African American
> representation in Harvard's incoming class would fall nearly one-third to
> approximately 10% of the class."
>
> — *Id.* **at 91**.

### File 03 — Card expert report

The original simulation behind the 14% → 6% finding. Card was Harvard's
economic expert. Paragraph 228:

> "In my simulation, the share of African-American students in the admitted
> class would drop from 14% to 6%. The fraction of Hispanic or Other students
> would fall from 14% to 9%. The fraction of admitted students who are
> Asian-American would rise from 24% to 27%. And the fraction of admitted
> students who are White would rise from 40% to 48%."
>
> — Card, *Expert Report*, [`03_card_expert_report.pdf`](03_card_expert_report.pdf) **¶ 228, at 103–104** (Dec. 15, 2017) (footnote and citation to Exhibit 36 omitted).

And the additional simulation that even removing legacy/donor/faculty-child
preferences does not restore Black enrollment:

> "If Harvard then applied a low-SES boost (as described above) and eliminated
> the practices alleged to benefit White applicants, the share of
> African-American students in the admitted class would be expected to remain
> constant at 6% — still radically below the current level."
>
> — *Id.* **¶ 239, at 108**.

### File 08 — Amherst / Williams et al. amicus brief

Two distinct forecasts in this brief: a research-based *aggregate* projection
for elite institutions, and an *Amherst-specific* forecast from Amherst's own
internal modeling (which is otherwise unpublished — this brief is the
original source).

Aggregate / industry-wide forecast (citing Bowen & Bok and Grodsky &
Kurlaender):

> "Black enrollment would likely decline between 50% and 70%; the probability
> of Black applicants receiving offers of admission would drop to half that of
> white students; and the percentage of Black students matriculating would
> drop from roughly 7.1% of the student body to 2.1%. Strictly enforced, a
> 'do not consider race' policy would 'presumably take [B]lack enrollments …
> back to early 1960s levels, before colleges and universities began to make
> serious efforts to recruit minority students.'"
>
> — Br. of Amici Curiae Amherst et al. Colleges and Bucknell et al.
> Universities, [`08_amherst_williams_amicus_brief.pdf`](08_amherst_williams_amicus_brief.pdf) **at 13** (Aug. 1, 2022) (citing Grodsky & Kurlaender (2006) and Bowen & Bok, *The Shape of the River* (1998)).

Amherst-specific forecast — the only public statement of Amherst's internal
findings:

> "Amherst, for example, has determined that an entirely race-blind policy
> would reduce the percentage of historically underrepresented students of
> color in its student body — including Native American, Black, and Hispanic
> students — by approximately half."
>
> — *Id.* **at 14**.

Williams-specific finding (qualitative):

> "After an exhaustive review of a range of race-neutral initiatives, Williams
> determined that none, either alone or in concert with one another, would,
> in the absence of race-conscious admissions policies, position Williams to
> achieve its diversity-related goals without significant and unacceptable
> sacrifice to other institutional priorities."
>
> — *Id.* **at 15**.

### File 09 — University of California Presidents' amicus brief

The UC system's chancellors and president use post-Proposition 209 outcomes
(California banned race-conscious admissions in 1996) as a forecast for what
elite peers should expect. The brief is signed by the UC President and the
chancellors of all 9 undergraduate campuses speaking in their official
capacity, so it is the most authoritative "presidential" forecast on record.

Aggregate finding:

> "Following the implementation of Proposition 209, the proportion of UC's
> enrollees from underrepresented minority groups dropped precipitously at
> UC, and dropped by 50% or more at UC's most selective campuses."
>
> — Br. of Amici Curiae President and Chancellors of the University of
> California, [`09_uc_presidents_amicus_brief.pdf`](09_uc_presidents_amicus_brief.pdf) **at 11** (Aug. 1, 2022).

Campus-level numbers (cut roughly in half within three years of the ban):

> "At UCLA, African American students made up 7.13% of the freshman class in
> 1995, and only 3.43% in 1998. … Latinx students were 21.58% of the freshman
> class in 1995, and only 10.45% in 1998. UC Berkeley's experience was
> similar. While African American students were 6.32% of the freshman class
> in 1995, they were 3.37% in 1998. And while Latinx students were 15.57% of
> the freshman class in 1995, they were only 7.28% in 1998."
>
> — *Id.* **at 12–13**.

### File 10 — Research universities amicus brief (Brown, Yale, Penn, et al.)

Filed by 15 of the wealthiest private research universities (Brown, Caltech,
Carnegie Mellon, Columbia, Cornell, Dartmouth, Duke, Emory, Johns Hopkins,
Princeton, UChicago, Penn, Vanderbilt, Washington University in St. Louis,
Yale). The brief is overwhelmingly qualitative — it argues that holistic
review is essential and that race-neutral alternatives would impair their
educational mission — but contains exactly **one** quantitative forecast, and
it is *borrowed* (a citation to the district court, not the universities'
own modeling):

> "[T]he record showed that the race-neutral alternatives proposed by
> Petitioner would lead to a near 33% reduction in the number of African
> American students admitted, absent other admissions policy changes that
> would 'result in a significant decline in the strength of Harvard's
> admitted classes across multiple dimensions, including its potential for
> academic and scholarly achievement.'"
>
> — Br. of Amici Curiae Brown University, et al., [`10_research_universities_amicus_brief.pdf`](10_research_universities_amicus_brief.pdf) **at 19–20** (Aug. 1, 2022) (citing District Court Pet. App. 220 — the Simulation D finding from file 01).

The brief otherwise gestures at the UC experience but only by reference to the
UC Presidents' *Fisher II* brief: "despite the implementation of numerous
race-neutral approaches aimed at enhancing diversity, 'the enrollment rates
for underrepresented minorities still have not rebounded at UC's most
selective campuses.'" *Id.* at 20.

**Notable absence.** Compare with file 08: the small liberal arts colleges
(Amherst, Williams, etc.) committed numerically to what would happen at
*their* institutions ("approximately half"). The 15 wealthiest research
universities did not. They had every resource to run race-blind simulations
on their own admissions data and either chose not to, or chose not to
disclose them in the brief. This contrast is itself a finding worth
flagging in any analysis: the most-resourced amici defended race-conscious
admissions without putting institutional numbers on the line.

### File 02 — First Circuit opinion

The First Circuit affirmed Burroughs and re-stated the same 14% → 6% finding:

> "If Harvard ceased considering race in its admissions process, the
> percentage of African American students enrolled would decrease from
> approximately 14% to 6%, and the percentage of Hispanic students would
> decline from 14% to 9%."
>
> — *SFFA v. President & Fellows of Harvard Coll.*, 980 F.3d 157, [`02_first_circuit_opinion_2020.pdf`](02_first_circuit_opinion_2020.pdf) (1st Cir. Nov. 12, 2020) (citing trial-court findings).

## Other quantitative forecasts by university leaders (statements not in this folder)

The most concrete *forecasts* on the record came from the universities' legal
filings (above). Public statements by individual presidents tended to be
qualitative; the few quantitative claims worth noting:

**Lee C. Bollinger** (then-President, Columbia University; the named
defendant in *Grutter v. Bollinger*) — drawing on the Michigan and California
experience:

> "I do think from the California and Michigan experience that there is a
> very significant decline in diversity, racial and ethnic diversity, and I
> think that will be the immediate consequence … I think we will fall short
> for many years to come, unfortunately."
>
> — Lee C. Bollinger, interview with Gothamist (June 30, 2023),
> [whats-next-after-affirmation-action-ruling-columbias-president-says-hes-worried](https://gothamist.com/news/whats-next-after-affirmation-action-ruling-columbias-president-says-hes-worried).

**The University of Michigan's experience as forecast** — cited by the UC
brief and elsewhere:

> "The percentage of Black students at the University of Michigan has
> decreased from 7% to 4%, and the percentage of Native American students
> dropped from 1 to 0.11 percent, a 90% reduction" since the state's 2006
> race-conscious admissions ban.
>
> — Summarized in [Crain's Detroit](https://www.crainsdetroit.com/politics-policy/how-michigan-colleges-diversify-without-affirmative-action) and [Bridge Michigan](https://bridgemi.com/talent-education/us-supreme-court-affirmative-action-ban-already-playing-out-michigan/) reporting on official U-M figures.

**Kevin Guskiewicz** (then-Chancellor, UNC Chapel Hill) — qualitative warning
on the Supreme Court steps after oral argument:

> "At Carolina, we are building better citizens, and if we remove race as one
> of the many factors we consider in admission decisions, we jeopardize that
> effort. Our students are preparing to enter diverse workforces, and if they
> do not have experience on group projects, or in their dorm rooms, with
> people who have different perspectives and life experiences, they will fall
> short of their potential."
>
> — UNC Chancellor Kevin Guskiewicz, statement at Supreme Court (Oct. 31,
> 2022), [WRAL](https://www.wral.com/unc-chancellor-speaks-after-supreme-court-arguments-on-affirmative-action/20548227/).

**Larry Bacow** (Harvard President 2018–2023) and **Christopher Eisgruber**
(Princeton President) — both issued public statements opposing SFFA and
defending diversity, but neither offered a *quantitative* forecast in any
located public statement; their schools' positions on numbers were carried by
the trial record (Card report, file 03) and the Amherst-led amicus brief
(file 08) respectively.

**Drew Faust** (Harvard President 2007–2018) testified at trial as the
final witness but offered no specific enrollment forecast on the record per
contemporaneous reporting; her testimony focused on diversity initiatives and
on the educational rationale rather than on numerical projections. See
[Harvard Crimson coverage](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2018/11/2/faust-testifies-in-admissions-trial/).
